Where robotaxis
sleep.

Robotaxi fleets in the US are growing city by city, and every one of those cars needs a place to park, charge and get serviced between rides. This brief pulls together what the open web says about that depot problem: who is building depots, what automated parking and charging cost, and which permits and codes stand in the way.

01 Takeaways

The short version, each one backed by sources further down.

  1. Robotaxi demand stopped being hypothetical in 2026.California approved Waymo across 18 counties in August, Nevada cleared Tesla, Uber and Waymo to run thousands of robotaxis, and Waymo launched in Denver, San Diego and Tampa.
  2. Depots, not software, are the next bottleneck.An industry writer calls real estate "one of the hardest open problems in scaled self-driving", and Lyft built an 80,000 sq ft depot in Nashville just to service, charge and maintain Waymo cars.
  3. Charging and cleaning are still done by hand.Waymo depot footage shows autonomous parking but manual charging and cleaning. A Reddit thread says Waymo is now preparing for autonomous charging.
  4. Automated parking costs about the same as concrete, in far less land.Per stall estimates run roughly $9,000 to $50,000 for automated systems against $25,000 to $55,000 for a conventional garage, with 50 to 80% less footprint (AI overview figures, verify before quoting).
  5. Codes are clear, but strict.The IBC and IFC require automatic sprinklers in mechanical-access enclosed garages, chargers are expected to be UL listed, and California runs a three-step AV permit ladder.

07 Codes and certification

A starting checklist for an automated parking and charging building in the US. Local adoption and amendments vary, so confirm with the jurisdiction.

  • Automatic sprinklers throughout mechanical-access enclosed parking garages. IBC and IFC section 903.2.10.2, and the area holding the mechanical garage needs a specially engineered system.ICCIBC 2021 ch. 9 · St Charles County adoption
  • Sprinklers for open parking garages above a fire-area threshold. New in the 2021 IFC.ICCIFC 2021
  • Fire apparatus access roads at least 20 ft wide. Unobstructed, excluding shoulders.ICCIFC 2024 ch. 5

08 What this means for our pitch

Our reading of the evidence above, not a finding from the sources.

  1. Lead with the depot, not the parking. In the US the buyer is a fleet operator like Lyft Flexdrive or Avomo, who needs dense parking with charging close to demand. A rotary tower with a charger in every bay is a compact depot.
  2. The decision layer has a dollar value. Depot energy only reaches $0.07 to $0.11 per kWh when charging is scheduled around demand charges and off-peak windows. Jev deciding charge now or wait, per car, is that saving.
  3. Automate the step that is still manual. Depots already park cars autonomously, but charging and cleaning are done by hand, and a Reddit thread says Waymo is preparing for autonomous charging. Docking into a bay that charges itself removes the plug-in step.
  4. Land is the argument, not price per stall. Automated systems cost about the same per stall as a concrete garage. The win is 50 to 80% less land near demand, which also cuts the non-revenue miles Terawatt describes.
  5. Build compliance in from day one. Sprinklers under IBC/IFC 903.2.10.2, UL listed charging, and fire access are table stakes. The escalate-when-unsure behaviour is a natural fit for safety events.
  6. Kerala first, US story second. The India charger costs above (₹4 to ₹12 lakh per DC unit) suit a Kochi or Kozhikode pilot. The US data shows the same tower can grow into robotaxi depots later.
